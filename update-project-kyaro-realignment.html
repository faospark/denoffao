Project Kyaro: Realignment.

October 25, 2026 • By Faospark • Project Direction

An important update on the future architecture, scope, and direction of Project Kyaro: why continuing to pile endless features into a single suite is no longer sustainable, how the mod will be reorganized into thematic packages, and what to expect next.

Today, I woke up excited to release a new version of Project Kyaro. But after spending some time reading through old notes, patch logs, and feedback, something finally clicked.

Project Kyaro has grown far beyond what it originally set out to be. At its current size, continuing to add feature after feature is no longer sustainable. More importantly, I feel like the project has reached a point where it needs a clearer direction instead of simply becoming a collection of everything I find interesting or useful. I guess that’s what you get when every month I am updating and adding 1 or two more features.

Unlike SuikodenFix, which has a very focused goal of improving gameplay, Project Kyaro has expanded into many different areas. While it started as a graphical and visual enhancement project, it has gradually evolved to include gameplay tweaks, battle-related changes, quality-of-life features, game-specific patches, and much more. As much as I enjoy adding new features, I've realized that Project Kyaro needs an identity of its own.

Executive Decision

Today, I made an executive decision to realign the future of the project.

While reviewing the most recent build, I noticed that the configuration file alone now contains 65 options. That doesn't even include the improvements that are permanently integrated into the mod and don't appear as configurable settings. I was also preparing to add roughly 10 more options before stopping to reconsider the direction I was taking.

The Presentation Dilemma

Looking at the Nexus Mods page from a new user's perspective, I realized that at first glance most people see Project Kyaro as a graphical mod. That is understandable, given how it is presented. The problem is that many users are probably unaware that it also contains gameplay-related features, such as the War Battle Patch and other non-graphical additions that have been introduced over time.

I suspect many players don't even realize some of these features exist because they are buried beneath a presentation that is primarily focused on visuals. No matter how many times I describe Project Kyaro as a "modding suite," the first impression people get is that it is a graphics mod, and first impressions tend to stick.

Because of that, I've decided to split Project Kyaro into multiple mods or at least move part of it to their own mods.

This decision is not about inflating my Nexus Mods library. Trust me, I already have plenty of mods. Instead, this is about making the project easier to maintain and making it easier for users to choose exactly what they want.

Some players may dislike certain components of the project, and that's perfectly understandable. There may even be users who avoid downloading Project Kyaro altogether because of one specific feature they don't want. By separating features into larger thematic groups, users can install only the parts that interest them.

Thematic Packages

To be clear, I am not planning to turn every small idea or feature into its own stand-alone mod. The goal is to group related features together based on a shared theme or purpose. If several features naturally fit under the same category, they will be combined into a dedicated package.

  • Graphics & UI Suite: High-definition portraits, textures, and interface assets.
  • Gameplay & Mechanics: Distinct battle-related enhancements, logic, and balance.
  • Modular Flexibility: Install only the modules you truly want.
Dedicated Standalone Releases

Of course, there will be exceptions. Some features have developed their own identity over time and deserve to stand on their own.

  • The Tenzan Patch: A well-known concept from years ago, now finally implemented properly, releasing as a dedicated stand-alone mod.
  • Targeted Patches: Dedicated standalone mods like the Kraken Patch for specific encounters.
  • Clean Maintenance: Independent versioning without config bloat.

So don't be surprised if I'll be releasing smaller mods just like the Kraken patch.

This realignment is ultimately about preserving my sanity as a developer, improving the maintainability of the project, and giving users greater flexibility in how they customize their experience.

Thank you for your understanding, and thank you for continuing to support Project Kyaro. I appreciate everyone who has followed the project, provided feedback, reported issues, and helped it grow into what it is today.

More updates will come as this new direction takes shape.
